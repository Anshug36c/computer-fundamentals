Module 5

Evolution and History of Computers

The story runs in two tracks: before electronic computers (calculating devices → Babbage) and the five generations (vacuum tubes → transistors → ICs → microprocessors → AI).

5.1 Why computers were invented — a one-line story

Humans needed to count, calculate, store and communicate faster and more accurately than hands and brains allow. So they invented calculating aids first (abacus, mechanical calculators), then machines that could follow stored instructions (Babbage, Hollerith), and finally fully electronic machines (ENIAC onwards) — which got smaller, cheaper, faster and more reliable with every generation.

5.2 Pre-history — the calculating aids (before electronic computers)

WhenInvention / PersonContribution — remember this line
~3000 BCAbacus (Babylon/China)First known manual calculating device; beads on rods; still used for teaching arithmetic. Called the first computer in many books — but it is manual, not automatic.
1617Napier's Bones — John NapierRods for multiplication & division; Napier also invented logarithms.
1622Slide Rule — William OughtredMechanical analog calculating device used by engineers for 300+ years.
1642Pascaline — Blaise PascalFirst mechanical calculator: gears, could add and subtract (built to help his father with tax work).
1673Stepped Reckoner — Gottfried LeibnizFirst machine that could multiply and divide; also gave us the binary number system (0 & 1).
1801Jacquard Loom — Joseph Marie JacquardUsed punched cards to control weaving patterns — the first idea of "instructions stored on cards" (weaving a picture = programming).
1822 / 1837Difference Engine & Analytical Engine — Charles BabbageCalled the "Father of the Computer". The Analytical Engine had a mill (CPU), store (memory), input (cards) and printer — the first general-purpose programmable design. It was never completed in his lifetime.
1843Ada LovelaceWrote the algorithm for the Analytical Engine to compute Bernoulli numbers → first computer programmer; foresaw that machines could work on music, not just numbers.
1890Herman HollerithPunched-card tabulating machine for the US Census (cut processing from 8 years to 2). His company later became IBM (1924, Thomas J. Watson).
1936–1950Alan TuringProposed the Turing Machine (mathematical model of a computer) and the concept of a universal programmable machine; cracked the Enigma code in WWII; in 1950 proposed the Turing Test for machine intelligence. Called the "father of computer science" / "father of AI".
1938–41Z1 / Z3 — Konrad Zuse (Germany)Z3 was the first programmable, fully automatic digital computer — using electromagnetic relays, not tubes.
1937–42ABC — Atanasoff & BerryFirst electronic digital computer (used vacuum tubes) but not fully programmable.
1943–44Colossus (UK)Electronic code-breaking machine used at Bletchley Park; not general purpose.
1944Harvard Mark-I — Howard Aiken & IBMElectro-mechanical general-purpose calculator; 50 feet long; used relay & mechanical parts.

5.3 The machines that start the "computer" story properly

MachineYearKey facts (high-yield for exams)
ENIAC
Electronic Numerical Integrator And Computer		1946Built by J. Presper Eckert & John Mauchly at the University of Pennsylvania. First large general-purpose electronic digital computer. Used ~18,000 vacuum tubes, weighed ~30 tonnes, occupied a room, did ~5,000 additions/second. Programming was done by re-wiring plugboards.
EDVAC
Electronic Discrete Variable Automatic Computer		Design 1945, built 1951Introduced the stored-program concept in the famous "First Draft of a Report on the EDVAC" by John von Neumann — both data and instructions live in the same memory. This is the von Neumann architecture used in virtually all computers today.
EDSAC1949Cambridge University (Maurice Wilkes) — the first practical stored-program computer put to regular work.
Manchester Baby (SSEM)1948First machine to actually run a program stored in electronic memory.
UNIVAC-I1951First commercial computer sold in the USA (Eckert–Mauchly / Remington Rand). Famously predicted the 1952 US election result from sample data.
IBM System/3601964First family of compatible computers (same software ran on small and large models) — a landmark of the 3rd generation.
Intel 40041971First microprocessor — a complete CPU on one chip (4-bit). Started the 4th generation and made the PC possible.
Altair 8800 / Apple II / IBM PC1975 / 1977 / 1981The personal-computer revolution. IBM PC made the PC an office standard; Microsoft supplied MS-DOS.

5.4 The Five Generations of Computers

Structure for a full-mark answer For each generation write: Period · Main technology · Features · Examples · Languages/Software. That's the standard 5-mark table.

First1940–56 Vacuum tubes (thermionic valves) Huge size, heavy, very costly, generated lots of heat, unreliable (tubes burnt out), consumed huge power, slow (milliseconds), machine-level programming, punched cards & paper tape, only one program at a time (batch). 5.6 India corner (bonus marks & good for viva)

  • 1955 — HEC-2M installed at the Indian Statistical Institute, Kolkata: the first electronic computer in India.
  • 1960 — TIFRAC, built at Tata Institute of Fundamental Research, Mumbai: India's first indigenously designed computer.
  • 1970s–80s — ECIL, HCL, Wipro and the param/ C-DAC era; "Param" means "supreme" in Sanskrit.
  • 1991 — PARAM 8000: India's first indigenous supercomputer, developed by C-DAC under Dr. Vijay Bhatkar. Led to National Supercomputing Mission (2015).
  • Today — PARAM series, PARAM Siddhi-AI (AI supercomputer) and the National Supercomputing Mission's PARAM Rudra systems power Indian research. India is also a global hub for software services (IT industry) and semiconductor/chip manufacturing is being expanded.
Module 6

Types of Computers

Three favourite classification bases: by data handling (analog/digital/hybrid), by size & power (super → micro), and by purpose (general / special). Learn the first and second in detail.

6.1 Classification by data handling (by the type of signal)

TypeWorks onWorking principleSpeed/AccuracyExamples
Analog computer Continuous physical quantities — voltage, pressure, temperature, rotation Measures and represents data by continuously varying physical quantities; gives results as graphs/dials Very fast (in real-time) but less accurate; output is approximate Speedometer, thermometer (mercury), analog watch, old slide rule, analog speed governor, oscilloscope, flight simulators, car "fuel gauge"
Digital computer Discrete data — counts, digits, 0s & 1s Works by counting; all data is converted to binary and processed logically with arithmetic circuits Very accurate; results in exact numbers; can store data; slower in real-time than analog but that gap is gone Laptop, desktop, smartphone, calculator, digital watch, ATM, server
Hybrid computer Both — accepts analog input, digitises it, processes digitally, may output analog Combines the speed of analog with the accuracy, memory and control of digital Accurate + real-time Aircraft/spacecraft control systems, ECG & ICU patient-monitoring machines, petrol pumps, hybrid speech synthesiser, weather & defence systems
Memory hook Analog = Analog is Approximate (continuous waves). Digital = Digital is Definite (0/1 digits). Hybrid = Hybrid is Half and half — the best of both.

6.4 Other modern classifications worth one line each

Embedded computer

A specialised computer built inside another device to control it — Washing machine, AC, car, router, smart TV.

Server

A computer that provides services/data to other computers (clients) over a network — web server, mail server, file server.

Workstation

High-performance single-user machine for engineering, design, video editing, scientific work.

Wearable / IoT / Edge device

Smartwatch, fitness band, smart sensor node; collects data and often sends it to the cloud.

Quantum computer

Uses qubits (superposition + entanglement) instead of bits; can explore many solutions at once for specific problems.

Grid / Cluster / Cloud

Many computers joined to work as one big resource; the cloud sells this as a service (IaaS/PaaS/SaaS).
