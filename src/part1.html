Each module starts simple and then goes exam-deep. Definitions are in bold, so you can revise by scanning for bold text.

Module 1

Data vs Information

Everything a computer does is about turning raw data into useful information. If you understand this one idea, the whole subject falls into place.

1.1 What is Data?

Data means raw, unorganised, unprocessed facts and figures that have little or no meaning by themselves. Data is the input to a computer.

  • Examples: 45, 78, 92, 67  ·  Rahul, 21, Kichha  ·  the letters typed on a keyboard, the pixels from a camera, the reading of a thermometer.
  • Data on its own does not tell you what it is about. Is 45 a temperature, a mark, an age or a price? You cannot say.
  • Data is factual, uninterpreted and often huge in quantity.

Types of data

BasisTypesMeaning & example
NatureQuantitativeNumeric, measurable — marks = 78, height = 172 cm
QualitativeDescriptive, non-numeric — "good student", "blue colour"
StructureStructuredRows and columns — a marksheet in Excel
UnstructuredNo fixed format — photos, videos, WhatsApp messages, audio
ContinuityDiscreteCountable values — number of students = 47
ContinuousAny value in a range — temperature = 31.62 °C

1.2 What is Information?

Information is data that has been processed, organised and given context so that it becomes meaningful and useful for decision making. Information is the output of a computer.

  • Example: the numbers 45, 78, 92, 67 become information when presented as "Rahul scored 78 out of 100 — Grade B, class average 70.5, position 3rd."
  • Information is processed, interpreted and decision-oriented.

Memory hook Data = Dumb facts. Information = Intelligent facts (data + meaning). Another classic line: "Data is processed to get information; information is data in context."

1.5 Qualities of good information

Marked as: accurate, complete, relevant, timely, clear, verifiable, cost-effective, accessible, reliable. Remember them with "ACRTVCCAR" or just the four big ones: ACCURATE · COMPLETE · RELEVANT · TIMELY.

Classic 5-mark question "With an example, distinguish between data and information." → Give 1 definition each, the difference table (any 4 points), then one clean example: marks of all students (data) → progress report with average and grade (information).
